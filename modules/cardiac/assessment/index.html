❤️ Cardiac

Chest pain differentials, ACS, heart failure, arrhythmias, hypertension, and ECG basics

Overview Assessment SOAP Note Resources Quiz

Cardiac History

❓

Chest Pain — Key Questions

  • Onset — sudden or gradual? What were you doing?
  • Location — central, left-sided, right-sided? Retrosternal?
  • Radiation — to jaw, left arm, shoulder, back, epigastrium?
  • Character — pressure, tightness, squeezing, burning, sharp, tearing?
  • Severity — 0–10 pain scale
  • Duration — seconds, minutes, hours, constant?
  • Relieving factors — GTN, rest, antacids, position, analgesia?
  • Aggravating factors — exertion, inspiration, palpation, eating?
  • Associated: dyspnoea, diaphoresis, nausea, syncope, palpitations?
  • Previous identical episodes?
📋

Cardiac Risk Factors

  • Age and sex (male >45, female >55 or post-menopausal)
  • Known IHD, previous MI, PCI, CABG
  • Hypertension — diagnosed and treatment
  • Hyperlipidaemia — LDL, total cholesterol
  • Diabetes — type, duration, glycaemic control
  • Smoking — pack-year history, current status
  • Family history — first-degree relative, premature CAD <55M/65F
  • Obesity and physical inactivity
  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Atrial fibrillation or other arrhythmia
💧

Heart Failure History

  • Dyspnoea — at rest, on exertion, NYHA class?
  • Orthopnoea — how many pillows?
  • Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
  • Ankle and leg oedema — onset, extent, pitting?
  • Exercise tolerance — how far can you walk?
  • Weight gain — recent rapid increase (fluid)?
  • Fatigue and reduced effort tolerance
  • Nocturia (increased renal perfusion when supine)
  • Known echo findings — EF, valvular disease
  • Current medications and compliance
〰️

Palpitations and Arrhythmia

  • Onset — sudden or gradual? What triggers?
  • Character — fast, slow, irregular, missed beats?
  • Duration — seconds to minutes or sustained?
  • Associated: dyspnoea, chest pain, presyncope, syncope?
  • Termination — spontaneous, Valsalva, position?
  • Caffeine, alcohol, exercise relationship
  • Thyroid disease, anaemia, electrolyte disturbance
  • Previous AF or arrhythmia diagnosis
  • Current anticoagulation or antiarrhythmic medications

Cardiovascular Examination

🔍

General and Vital Signs

  • Appearance — distress, diaphoresis, pallor, cyanosis
  • BP — both arms (dissection screen), lying and standing
  • HR — rate, rhythm, character
  • RR — tachypnoea suggests decompensation
  • SpO2 — on air and with supplemental oxygen
  • Temperature — fever may indicate infective endocarditis
  • BMI and waist circumference
  • 12-lead ECG — perform early in all chest pain
👁️

Peripheral Signs

  • Hands — clubbing, splinter haemorrhages, peripheral cyanosis
  • Eyes — xanthelasma, corneal arcus, anaemia, Roth spots
  • Face — malar flush (mitral stenosis)
  • Neck — JVP height and waveform, carotid bruits
  • Legs — peripheral oedema (pitting, extent), varicosities
  • Calves — DVT signs (PE risk)
  • Peripheral pulses — femoral, popliteal, pedal
🫀

Precordial Examination

  • Inspection — visible pulsations, scars (sternotomy, pacemaker)
  • Palpation — apex beat location and character
  • Palpation — heaves and thrills
  • Auscultation — S1 and S2 character and splitting
  • Added sounds — S3 (HF), S4 (stiff ventricle), clicks
  • Murmurs — timing, location, radiation, character, grade
  • Lung bases — bilateral crackles suggest pulmonary oedema
📊

Investigations to Order

  • 12-lead ECG — always first in chest pain or palpitations
  • High sensitivity troponin — 0h and 2h (or 0h and 3h)
  • FBE — anaemia, infection
  • EUC — renal function, electrolytes
  • BGL and HbA1c
  • Fasting lipids — total, LDL, HDL, TG
  • TFTs — hyperthyroidism causes AF and palpitations
  • BNP or NT-proBNP — heart failure diagnosis and monitoring
  • CXR — cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema, pleural effusion
  • Echo — if new murmur, HF suspected, or post-ACS

Risk Scoring Tools

🎯

HEART Score (Chest Pain)

  • H — History: highly suspicious (2), moderately (1), slightly (0)
  • E — ECG: LBBB/ST changes (2), non-specific (1), normal (0)
  • A — Age: ≥65 (2), 45–64 (1), <45 (0)
  • R — Risk factors: ≥3 or atherosclerosis (2), 1–2 (1), none (0)
  • T — Troponin: >3x normal (2), 1–3x (1), normal (0)
  • Score 0–3: low risk (1.7% MACE) — consider discharge
  • Score 4–6: moderate risk — observe, serial troponins
  • Score 7–10: high risk — early invasive strategy
🩸

NYHA Functional Class (Heart Failure)

  • Class I — No symptoms with ordinary activity
  • Class II — Slight limitation, comfortable at rest, symptoms with moderate exertion
  • Class III — Marked limitation, comfortable at rest, symptoms with minimal exertion
  • Class IV — Symptoms at rest, inability to carry on any activity
  • Used to guide therapy titration and referral
  • NYHA III–IV → urgent cardiology referral
