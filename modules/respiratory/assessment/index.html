Clinical Module

🫁 Respiratory

Dyspnoea differentials, asthma, COPD, pneumonia, PE, and spirometry interpretation

Overview Assessment SOAP Note Resources Quiz

Respiratory History

❓

Dyspnoea — Key Questions

  • Onset — sudden (PE, pneumothorax) or gradual (COPD, malignancy)?
  • Duration — seconds, hours, days, or weeks?
  • Severity — at rest, on exertion, or both? MRC dyspnoea scale?
  • Character — tight, heavy, cannot get enough air?
  • Positional — worse lying flat (orthopnoea = cardiac)?
  • Triggering factors — allergens, cold air, exercise, viral URTI?
  • Associated wheeze — expiratory or inspiratory?
  • Associated cough — productive or dry? Sputum colour and volume?
  • Haemoptysis — volume, frequency, clots?
  • Chest pain — pleuritic (PE, pleuritis) or pressure (cardiac)?
  • Fever and systemic symptoms — infection?
  • Night sweats and weight loss — malignancy or TB?
📋

Respiratory Background History

  • Previous respiratory diagnoses — asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis
  • Previous spirometry results and trend
  • Current inhalers — names, doses, technique, adherence
  • Smoking history — pack years, current status, cessation attempts
  • Occupational exposures — dust, fumes, asbestos, silica, birds
  • Childhood respiratory illness — recurrent croup, bronchiolitis
  • Atopy — eczema, allergic rhinitis, food allergy
  • Family history of asthma, COPD, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
  • Vaccinations — influenza, pneumococcal, COVID
  • Travel history — TB risk, melioidosis (tropical Australia)
  • VTE risk factors — immobility, surgery, malignancy, OCP, pregnancy
📊

Asthma Control Assessment

  • Daytime symptoms — frequency per week?
  • Reliever use — how many times per week?
  • Nocturnal symptoms or waking?
  • Activity limitation due to asthma?
  • Any oral corticosteroid courses in past 12 months?
  • Any ED presentations or hospitalisations?
  • Written asthma action plan — do they have and understand one?
  • Inhaler technique — observe and correct
  • Trigger identification and avoidance
💨

COPD Assessment Tools

  • mMRC Dyspnoea Scale (0–4) — functional limitation
  • CAT Score — 8 questions, guides treatment escalation
  • Exacerbation frequency — how many in last 12 months?
  • Previous hospitalisations for COPD?
  • Home oxygen — current prescription and hours of use?
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation — completed or referred?
  • Nutritional status — weight loss, muscle wasting in severe COPD

Respiratory Examination

🔍

General and Vital Signs

  • SpO2 — on air first, then on oxygen if applicable
  • RR — count for a full 60 seconds
  • HR and BP — tachycardia in PE, sepsis, severe asthma
  • Temperature — fever suggests infection
  • General — respiratory distress, accessory muscle use, position
  • Speech — sentences, phrases, or words?
  • Cyanosis — peripheral (nails) or central (lips, tongue)
  • Pursed-lip breathing — classic in COPD
  • PEFR — peak expiratory flow rate if asthma suspected
👁️

Inspection and Palpation

  • Chest shape — barrel chest (hyperinflation in COPD), kyphoscoliosis
  • Chest wall movement — symmetrical? Paradoxical?
  • Trachea — central or deviated (tension pneumothorax, effusion)?
  • Intercostal recession — sign of increased work of breathing
  • Expansion — assess anteriorly and posteriorly, compare sides
  • Tactile fremitus — increased (consolidation), decreased (effusion)
  • Peripheral signs — clubbing (malignancy, bronchiectasis), oedema
  • Lymphadenopathy — cervical, axillary, supraclavicular
🔊

Percussion and Auscultation

  • Percussion — resonant (normal), dull (consolidation, effusion), hyper-resonant (pneumothorax)
  • Breath sounds — vesicular, bronchial (consolidation), absent (effusion, pneumothorax)
  • Wheeze — expiratory (obstructive), inspiratory and expiratory (severe obstruction)
  • Crackles — fine late inspiratory (fibrosis, early pulmonary oedema), coarse (consolidation, bronchiectasis)
  • Pleural rub — pleuritis or PE
  • Vocal resonance — increased (consolidation), decreased (effusion)
  • Cardiac auscultation — exclude cardiac cause of dyspnoea
📊

Investigations to Order

  • SpO2 and arterial blood gas if SpO2 <94% or severe distress
  • CXR — all new dyspnoea, suspected pneumonia, effusion, malignancy
  • Spirometry — diagnosis and monitoring of asthma and COPD
  • PEFR — acute asthma assessment and monitoring
  • FBE — anaemia, eosinophilia (asthma), infection
  • CRP and procalcitonin — infection vs non-infective
  • D-dimer — low probability PE (Wells ≤4)
  • CTPA — if high probability PE or elevated D-dimer
  • Sputum MC&S — pneumonia, COPD exacerbation
  • Respiratory viral panel — if viral aetiology suspected
  • FeNO — eosinophilic airway inflammation in asthma
